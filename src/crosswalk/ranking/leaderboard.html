Where is the safest place on Earth to cross the street right now?

Checking the footage…

A deliberately absurd question, answered carefully: this page compares observable pedestrian–vehicle indicators in recorded footage. It is an experiment, not a measure of risk and not safety advice.

Distances in metres from a hand-set calibration. Ranked by the upper end of the 95% interval for close approaches per 100 pedestrian-minutes, lowest first, so a crossing cannot win on a short, lucky observation.

Uncalibrated crossings (apparent, in pixels)

No calibration yet, so "close" is judged against the pedestrian's apparent size in the image. These numbers are not comparable with the table above, or between cameras.

Not ranked yet

Crossings with too little footage to say anything with confidence.

What this cannot tell you

  • It is not risk. A close approach is a pedestrian and a vehicle being near each other on camera. Almost none lead to harm, and the page knows nothing about who had right of way.
  • The footage is recorded. "Right now" means when the numbers were computed, not when the video was filmed. One clip shows one period on one day.
  • The detector makes mistakes. Missed people (small, hidden, at night), false detections and broken tracks change every count. Riders on bikes may be counted twice.
  • Pixels are not metres. Without calibration, distances depend on the camera's height and angle. With it, four hand-picked points are exact only near themselves.
  • Sampling misses moments. Frames are sampled a few times a second; the true closest approach can fall between them.
  • Small numbers are noisy. That is why every rate carries an interval and crossings with little footage are not ranked.
